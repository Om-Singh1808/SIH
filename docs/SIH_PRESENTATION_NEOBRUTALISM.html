💡 Why Sampling Rate (2–5 fps) Unlocks Scale

In retail analytics, humans move at ~1.2 m/s and shelf stockouts persist for minutes. Full 30 fps processing is wasteful. Sampling frames at 4 fps reduces compute overhead by 86%, allowing a budget Raspberry Pi 5 to analyze multiple camera streams concurrently without overheating.

⚙️ Threading & Async Execution Model
  • Dedicated CV Worker Threads: OpenCV/ONNX runs in OS worker threads (one per camera) releasing Python GIL during native execution. Prevents video reading from blocking REST APIs.
  • Single-Writer Asyncio Loop: All SQLite WAL write operations are funneled through a single asyncio thread. Eliminates database lock contention (`database is locked`).
  • Explicit Backpressure Queue: Bounded buffer (`maxsize=1000`) drops oldest telemetry frames if consumer slows down, ensuring alerts are never lost.

Offline-First Integrity: "Cable Kaat Do" Protocol

Proven store-and-forward architecture with gap-free sequence verification

🔒 Transactional Outbox Guarantees

Observations and outbox rows are committed in **one single SQLite transaction**:

BEGIN IMMEDIATE; INSERT INTO events (event_id, seq, payload, ts); INSERT INTO outbox (id=seq, topic, expires_ts, sent_ts=NULL); COMMIT;

If power is cut mid-transaction, SQLite WAL rolls back cleanly. `seq` numbers remain 100% gap-free.

📖 The 0.31 Gruen Factor Basis

Based on global retail research by Gruen, Corsten & Bharadwaj (2002) (71,000 shoppers across 29 countries): 31% of shoppers leave the store without purchasing when their intended item is out of stock. RetailSense uses this exact factor to compute monetary risk.

💰 Recovered Rupee Calculation

When store staff restocks after an alert, RetailSense measures actual gap vs. an unattended 2-hour baseline:

Saved = ₹301 (unattended 2h) - ₹20 (actual 8 min gap) = ₹281 SAVED

This number is directly incremented on the shopkeeper's "₹ बचाया आज" card.

🔒 Privacy Architecture Safeguards
  • Zero Face Recognition: The system strictly detects person bounding boxes. No facial recognition or biometric scanning models exist in the codebase.

❓ Q1: What accuracy do you actually achieve?

Answer: All metrics are asserted in CI against synthetic ground truth (`sim.truth`): Footfall within ±10% of true crossings (`test_e2e_synthetic`), shelf coverage within ±0.1 of true capacity, and queue forecast holdout MAE ≤ 0.8 customers against a naive baseline (matching Cali Intelligence benchmarks).

❓ Q2: Why not run everything in the cloud?

Answer: Three reasons: 1) 85% of Indian stores face daily power cuts; a cloud system fails when the store is busiest. 2) Streaming 4K video from 12M stores consumes prohibitive bandwidth. 3) Local edge inference costs electricity, not GPU hours, making ₹299/mo pricing viable.

❓ Q3: How is this original vs Ultralytics samples?

Answer: Ultralytics provides raw detector primitives. Our contribution is the business layer above: ₹ impact strings with cited citations, 3-scan persistence with occlusion skip, Little's Law on measured cashier speed, offline transactional outbox replay, and Hindi WhatsApp ops loops.

❓ Q4: What breaks first in production honestly?

Answer: Cluttered multi-SKU shelves where classical vision sees "covered" even if wrong product fills the gap. We mitigate this by plugging a learned empty-shelf detector (Sensors 2024 model) behind the exact same `CoverageEstimator` interface for P1 release.
